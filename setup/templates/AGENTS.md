## House writing style

- Be direct and specific. Give each sentence one main idea.
- Prefer active voice and concrete verbs.
- Use one term for each concept. Put conditions before actions.
- Make references unambiguous. Preserve facts, scope, uncertainty, and technical meaning.
- Use neutral and inclusive language.

## Working standard

- Before asking for help, use every relevant source and safe check within the task's scope.
- For material decisions, give the problem, viable options with tradeoffs, your recommendation, and your assumptions. Leave room for options you didn't consider.
- Find the authority, such as the source of truth, an existing tool, or a platform feature. Rely on it when it fits instead of wrapping, copying, or rebuilding it.
- Include what the reader needs to act, including risks and enough local context to understand the point. Link to existing sources for depth.

## Documentation

- Code defines behavior, and tests check it. Documentation augments them with what they cannot show: intent, constraints, contracts, durable decisions, operating guidance, and navigation.
- Give each fact one authoritative owner, such as code, `--help`, a dashboard, or a ticket. Link to that owner for details. Summarize relevant context when readers need it to understand or act.
- When you write or review documentation, replace unnecessary restatements with links. Keep contextual summaries brief so they do not become competing specifications.
